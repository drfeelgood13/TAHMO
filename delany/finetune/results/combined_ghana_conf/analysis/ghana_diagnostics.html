Ghana Test-Set Diagnostics

What the reference fields and the predicted fields look like, and which of their characteristics go with good or poor delineation. Model: Delineate Anything v2 fine-tuned on Ghana + VIEDIA at 1 m (combined_1m), run on the 32 Ghana test images.

32 images1,534 reference fields2,319 predicted polygons regions north (21), ej (11)field F1 0.211

Key findings

  1. Field size matters most. Only 2% of the smallest quarter of fields (under about 740 m², roughly 27 × 27 m) are found, against 44% of the largest quarter. Small fields are mostly absorbed into bigger predictions (56% merged); the largest fields are mostly broken into pieces (66% split).
  2. Most fields are seen, but not outlined as one piece. On average 78% of each reference field's area lies under some prediction, yet only 27% are matched one-to-one at IoU ≥ 0.5. The gap is splitting (24% of fields) and merging (43%), not missing fields.
  3. Rice is easiest, mixed small crops are hardest. Rice fields are found 49% of the time: they are greener, smoother and have the sharpest edges. "Other crops" (sorghum, groundnut, yam, vegetables…) are found only 14% of the time: they are the smallest group with the weakest edges.
  4. Label quality shows in the scores. Fields labelled with high confidence are found 32% of the time; low-confidence or unlabelled-confidence fields only 17%–21%. "Unidentified" and "fallow" fields are found less often than maize and are merged more often (56% of unidentified fields).
  5. 26% of the predicted polygons fall on the non-crop background (607 of 2,319). Another 45% lie inside real fields but don't match them: pieces of split fields. Only 18% of predictions match a field.
  6. Images with dense, regular field patterns score best. Across images, F1 rises with fields per hectare (ρ = +0.38) and falls with the share of non-crop land (ρ = -0.36). Colour and brightness of the image matter little.
  7. A higher confidence cut-off does not help. False detections on the background have slightly lower confidence (median 0.20 against 0.25 for matched fields), but the groups overlap so much that raising the cut-off removes good fields almost as fast as bad ones. F1 is highest at the current cut-off of 0.15.

Tile-level accuracy (mAP)

These scores judge the model on its own, tile by tile, before the delineation pipeline joins tiles into maps. mAP50 summarises precision and recall over every confidence cut-off, counting a field as found at IoU ≥ 0.5; mAP50-95 averages stricter overlap thresholds up to 0.95, so it rewards tight outlines. Precision, recall and F1 here are at the cut-off where F1 is highest for that model. combined_1m raises mask mAP50 from 0.147 (v2 without fine-tuning) to 0.249, a 69% gain.

What information exists

The predicted polygons carry their outline, area and the model's confidence. Confidence comes from a re-run with save_detections: true that reproduced the original results exactly. Everything else below is joined in from the labels, the images and the scores.

Labels (GPS survey)
Crop (crop: maize, rice, other, noncrop) and detailed crop (crop1: 24 values incl. fallow and unidentified), label confidence (high / medium / low / none), region (north, ej), survey date (15 Sep 2021 in the north, 15 Dec 2021 in ej, a few others).
Images (1 m RGB)
Measured per field: mean colour, brightness, greenness (VARI, from −1 to 1; higher is greener), texture (spread of brightness inside the field) and edge contrast (brightness difference between the field and a 3 m ring around it). Image acquisition dates are not available; colours describe the imagery, not the survey day.
Scores
Per image: precision, recall, F1, area omission/commission, boundary F1, PoLiS. Per field: matched at IoU ≥ 0.5, best IoU, area covered, split, merged.
Model confidence
Per predicted polygon: the confidence of the raw detection that matches it best (0.15 to 1; the pipeline drops detections below 0.15), plus the highest confidence among the detections it was built from.
Not available
Image dates, crop height or growth stage.

How to read the outcome columns: Found = matched one-to-one with IoU ≥ 0.5 · Area covered = share of the field under any prediction · Split = covered by two or more predictions · Merged = its main prediction also covers another field.

Field size

Below about 740 m² the model almost never draws a field on its own: such fields are usually swallowed by a larger polygon. Above about 6,000 m² fields are found most often, but two thirds of them are also cut into several pieces, which costs both precision and the one-to-one match. Both effects point at the delineation settings (minimum area, merge thresholds) as much as at the model.

The greenest quarter is found least often (17%, against 28%–33% for the others) and is covered least. These are probably tall or dense crops, or fields next to trees and bush, where the boundary is hidden by vegetation. Rough, patchy fields (high texture) are found less often and split more. Sharp edges help: fields that stand out from their surroundings are found more often and merged less. Irregular, elongated fields (low compactness) are found least often. Brightness on its own makes little difference.

What the predicted polygons are

Only 18% of predictions are a clean match. Almost half are pieces of real fields: the model sees those fields, but draws them in parts. 26% sit on the non-crop background; these are the real false detections according to the labels. They are darker than the other predictions and have weaker edges than the matched ones. The predictions that merge several fields are the largest (median 6,640 m²).

Model confidence

For every field it draws, the model gives a confidence between 0 and 1. The pipeline keeps everything at or above 0.15. If false detections had clearly lower confidence than correct ones, a higher cut-off would remove them cheaply.

What this suggests

  1. Tune the delineation settings before retraining. Splitting of large fields and merging of small ones are the two biggest losses, and both are driven by the merge thresholds and minimum areas in the config. Tune them on the validation images.
  2. Decide what counts as a field. Unidentified and low-confidence polygons behave differently from the clearly labelled crops. Scoring with and without them shows how much of the gap is label definition.
  3. Look at the greenest and the smallest fields in QGIS. They are where the model fails most; a visual check will tell whether the boundaries are visible at 1 m at all.
  4. Keep the confidence cut-off at 0.15, or test a lower one. Raising it trades recall for precision at a loss in F1 (see Model confidence). Since F1 still falls as the cut-off rises from 0.15, a cut-off of 0.10 is worth one run on the validation images.
